What is different

  • Parser: the shared parser reads numeric dates month-first even when the tenant's date convention is day-first. In R-41203, an app booking for 2 December appears in the invite as 12 February.
  • Form hint: booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY, but for a day-first tenant its form interprets the entry day-first. In R-41259, a form booking for 11 April appears as 4 November. The visible instruction and interpretation must agree; this is not evidence that the shared parser alone caused this report.

These mechanisms come from the supplied KB logic and tenant data. The production parser and form were not supplied; reproductions against the actual product remain to be performed. The computed assignment is useful for triage, not proof that every report individually demonstrates the assigned mechanism.

What the desk should do next

  1. Check the report body and the tenant's configured locale and form version. Compare expected and actual behavior, including how the date entered the system.
  2. Match a report to a demonstrated mechanism, not just a similar subject, browserLocale, or intakeTag. App/email reports and form reports can differ even for the same tenant.
  3. Track the shared-parser and booking-v3 hint problems in separate issues. Keep the original report IDs and the snapshot date with each count.
  4. Keep unexplained reports visibly unresolved pending evidence. Do not infer another defect solely from a city name, weekday, or the desk's note label.
  5. Before claiming resolution, verify the product flow, record the fix and check the disposition of the flagged reports. Passing tests of this KB classifier only verifies the KB, not the production product.

For example, R-41211 and R-41251 share a tenant and title, but the app/form difference changes their assignment. R-41256 and R-41428 even share the same body and channel; different tenants and form versions put them in different groups.
